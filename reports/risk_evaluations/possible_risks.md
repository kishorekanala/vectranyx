# Possible Risks — ECRA System Evaluation

This document lists possible risks to consider when evaluating a target enterprise (for lending or investment) using the ECRA System. Grouped by category with brief descriptions and evidence pointers.

## 1. Strategic & Market Risks
- Market Demand Risk: Insufficient or falling customer demand; inaccurate TAM/SAM/SOM assumptions.
- Competitive Risk: Strong incumbents, low-entry competitors, or rapid innovation reducing differentiation.
- Product-Market Fit Risk: Misaligned value proposition or poor early-adopter validation (failed pilots, no LOIs).

## 2. Supply Chain & Operations Risks
- Single‑source Supplier Risk: Critical component or material is single-sourced.
- Lead-time Volatility: Long or unpredictable supplier lead times causing production gaps.
- Logistics & Distribution Risk: Transit bottlenecks, customs/tariff exposure, or inadequate distribution partners.
- Capacity & Quality Risk: Manufacturing or service delivery unable to scale with acceptable quality.

## 3. Financial & Capital Structure Risks
- Liquidity Runway Risk: Cash runway too short under base or stress scenarios.
- Leverage & Covenant Risk: High leverage or covenant proximity (breach probability under stress).
- Revenue Concentration Risk: Overdependence on a few customers or contracts.
- Receivables / Working Capital Risk: Long DSO, payment delays, or weak collections.

## 4. Pricing, Margin & Unit Economics Risks
- Margin Compression Risk: Input-cost pass-through inability, supplier price hikes, or pricing pressure.
- Unit-Economics Fragility: Long CAC payback, negative contribution margins, or poor LTV/CAC.

## 5. Regulatory, Legal & Compliance Risks
- Licensing & Permit Risk: Missing or delayed regulatory approvals, sector-specific licenses.
- Regulatory Change Risk: Policy or subsidy rollbacks, tariff changes, or new compliance burdens.
- Litigation & Contract Risk: Pending lawsuits, weak contract terms, or unenforceable agreements.
- IP Risk: Unclear ownership, weak protection, or potential infringement.

## 6. ESG & Ethical Risks
- Environmental Risk: Carbon tax exposure, regulatory constraints, or high emissions footprint.
- Labor & Supply‑chain Compliance: Labor law violations, modern slavery risks, or weak supplier audits.
- Ethical / Social Risk: Products or business models with harmful externalities or reputational sensitivity.

## 7. National Security & Strategic Risks
- Dual‑use Technology Risk: Exposure to technologies with export controls or national-security scrutiny.
- Foreign Ownership / Sanctions Risk: Sensitive ownership structures or exposure to sanctioned parties.
- Critical Infrastructure Dependence: Reliance on regulated grids, ports, or telecom assets.

## 8. People & Governance Risks
- Key‑person Risk: Loss of founders or critical leaders without succession plans.
- Governance Weakness: Conflicts of interest, weak board oversight, or poor financial controls.
- Talent & Hiring Risk: Inability to recruit required skills at planned pace/cost.

## 9. Technology, Data & Cybersecurity Risks
- Technical Feasibility Risk: Overambitious technical milestones or underestimated engineering effort.
- Data Privacy & Compliance: Non-compliance with data protection laws (GDPR/local) or poor data hygiene.
- Cybersecurity Risk: Weak security posture, lack of incident response, or critical vulnerabilities.

## 10. Counterparty & Concentration Risks
- Bank / Lender Concentration: Funding lines concentrated with a single provider.
- Customer / Supplier Concentration: Loss of a major counterparty materially impacts cashflow.

## 11. Macro & Market Risks
- Macro Shock Risk: Interest-rate spikes, currency devaluation, inflation, or economic contraction.
- Commodity & Input Price Risk: Volatile raw-material prices (copper, steel, energy).

## 12. Insurance & Liability Risks
- Inadequate Coverage: Missing product liability, D&O, or business-interruption insurance.
- High‑severity Low‑probability Risks: Catastrophic events without sufficient transfer mechanisms.

## 13. Reputational & Strategic Execution Risks
- Reputational Damage: Public controversies, product failures, or regulatory fines affecting sales.
- Execution Risk: Overly aggressive timelines, supplier onboarding failures, or partner breakdowns.

## Evidence & Mitigation Pointers
- Evidence: financial model, contracts, pilot reports, supplier agreements, regulatory filings, IP filings, insurance policies, and CVs.
- Mitigations: dual-sourcing, inventory buffers, milestone‑based tranching, covenants, escrowed collateral, insurance, governance fixes, and targeted audits.
